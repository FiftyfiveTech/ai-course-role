Why the Evaluator Model Must Differ from the Persona Model

Self-preference bias — what it is, why it matters, and the rule that follows.

1. The Problem: Self-Preference Bias

When an LLM is asked to judge two outputs — one it generated itself and one from a different model — it systematically rates its own output higher, even when the other output is objectively better by human standards.

This is not a subtle effect. Studies have observed 10–30 percentage-point inflation in win-rates when a model scores its own text versus human preference judgements on the same pairs.

⚠ Key Finding
If the same model family both plays the persona and grades the trainee's response to that persona, the scores are corrupted — the evaluator rewards replies that match the persona model's style, not replies that are actually good at the coaching skill being trained.

Why it happens

  • Training distribution alignment. A model assigns higher likelihood to text that resembles its own training and generation patterns. Higher likelihood maps to higher perceived quality.
  • Style familiarity. Each model family has idiosyncratic phrasing, hedging patterns, and answer structure. A judge model that shares those patterns reads them as fluent and authoritative.
  • No ground truth anchor. Without a human reference or explicit rubric grounded outside the model's own style, the model defaults to comparing against its internal reference — itself.

2. What the Evidence Shows

Setting Self-score win-rate Human win-rate Inflation
GPT-4 judging GPT-4 vs GPT-3.5 outputs ~70 % ~58 % +12 pp
Claude judging Claude vs Llama outputs ~68 % ~54 % +14 pp
Llama-3 judging Llama-3 vs Mistral outputs ~65 % ~50 % +15 pp

Illustrative figures drawn from the LLM-as-judge literature (Zheng et al. 2023, Panickssery et al. 2024). Exact numbers vary by task and prompt.

The pattern holds across model families and scales with how different the model families are: same family → large bias, different families → smaller bias.

3. Why It Matters for This Project

In the ROLE coaching loop: 

Persona  (plays customer)      →  openai/gpt-oss-120b  via Groq
Trainee  (human or agent)      →  responds to the persona
Evaluator (scores the trainee) →  ???

If the evaluator is openai/gpt-oss-120b — the same model as the persona — it will inflate scores for trainee replies that sound like things openai/gpt-oss-120b would say. A trainee who mimics the persona's style gets a free points boost. A trainee who uses a different but equally effective style gets penalised.

The scorecard becomes a measure of style similarity to the persona, not coaching skill.

⚠ Concrete failure mode
Persona says: "I placed an order last Tuesday and it still hasn't shipped."
Trainee A (mirrors persona style): "I understand your order from last Tuesday hasn't arrived yet — let me pull that up."
Trainee B (different but effective): "Got it — order placed Tuesday, not shipped. Let me check the fulfilment queue."

A same-model evaluator scores A higher. A human coach may prefer B for efficiency.

4. The Fix: Use a Different Model Family

The evaluator must come from a different model family than the persona. This breaks the self-preference circuit because the evaluator has no in-distribution preference for the persona's stylistic patterns.

Role Model (HF repo id) Provider
Persona openai/gpt-oss-120b Groq (free tier)
Evaluator meta-llama/Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct Groq (free tier)

Llama-3.3-70B is from a completely different training lineage than the OpenAI GPT family, so it has no in-distribution preference for GPT-style phrasing. It grades on rubric compliance, not style match.

What "different" means in practice

  • Different base model family — not just a fine-tune of the same base.
  • Different training data mix — ideally different organisations' RLHF pipelines.
  • Never the model playing the persona — even a larger version of the same model carries bias.
Project Rule — Evaluator ≠ Persona
The evaluator model must come from a different model family than the persona model.
Violation: evaluator is any variant of openai/gpt-oss-* while persona is also openai/gpt-oss-*.

This rule is enforced by test tests/gates/test_no_self_eval.py (written in ROLE-009).
